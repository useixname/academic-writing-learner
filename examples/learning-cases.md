# Learning cases

## Comparable-paper prevalence beats one-paper repetition

**Corpus:** Fifteen comparable introductions. A phrase appears 27 times, but all occurrences are in two papers and sixteen are in one paper.

**Learning record:** `paper_prevalence: 2/15`; `raw_occurrence_count: 27`; `classification: author-specific`; `confidence: Low for a corpus convention`.

**Lesson:** Report the concentration. Do not call the phrase “very common in the field.”

## Captions are conditionally useful

**Request:** Learn how the sources introduce figures in the Results section.

**Action:** Include figure captions and nearby explanatory prose because they directly serve the requested task. Exclude isolated axis labels and table cells from normal sentence and word counts.

**Lesson:** Ingestion is task-aware, but excluded material does not silently become corpus evidence.

## A reusable move with conditions

**Observed:** Four of five supplied introductions acknowledge a concrete advance and then identify a limitation that follows despite that advance.

**Record:** `local_role: state-limitation`; `pattern: acknowledge relevant progress before naming the remaining limitation`; `appropriate_when: genuine progress and a genuine remaining problem`; `avoid_when: no progress has been established or the next statement is not a limitation`; `transfer_level: conceptual`; `confidence: High`.

**Lesson:** The learned unit is the move and its conditions, not a source sentence.
